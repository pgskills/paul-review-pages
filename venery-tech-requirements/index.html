Venery Technology Requirements

What the system needs to do, and what it needs to remember about each member and event — before any conversation about which platform does it.

Founder — Alex Stokman
Prepared with Paul Gladen
2026‑09‑07 · v1

What this is: a requirements list, not a product recommendation. Nothing here names a platform or compares options. The goal is to agree on what the system needs to do and what it needs to remember, so that whatever gets evaluated next (existing tools, a different combination of them, or something custom-built) gets judged against the same list.

Two levels: Part 1 is business and member requirements in plain language, the kind of thing to react to and correct. Part 2 gets more specific: the actual pieces of information the system would need to track, and the everyday sequences (someone registers, someone joins, someone refers a friend) those pieces of information have to support. Part 2 is where today's real problems, like an event registrant who never shows up as a subscriber, actually get solved or not.

PART 1 Business & Member Requirements

Two different audiences, two different lists. Some things (the website) serve both and aren't forced into either column.

Foundational

  • Public-facing website — program information, marketing pages. Serves both audiences; not treated as "business" or "member" on its own.

Business What Venery needs to run itself

  • One customer record per person, not one per system.Directly from Alex's own notes: an event registrant who doesn't subscribe is invisible to Klaviyo, and Wix never passes registration data like address into a customer profile at all. Both are the same underlying gap.
  • Event & program registration, with payment.Currently Wix Events.
  • Two kinds of payment, handled differently: one-time (tickets, merch) and recurring (membership dues).Recurring billing brings renewals, failed payments, and proration, none of which one-time ticket sales need to solve.
  • Email marketing & lifecycle automation.Currently Klaviyo.
  • Relationship & activity tracking, separate from marketing.Klaviyo is a genuine CRM in the sense that it centralizes customer data and lifecycle messaging. By its own positioning, it doesn't track pipelines, relationship history, or ongoing activity. That's the specific gap: nowhere to record "we're in exploratory talks with this instructor" or "this vendor asked for different terms last month."
  • Membership administration: tiers, access control, renewals, discount eligibility.
  • Instructor management: who's available, what they teach, how they're compensated — tied to specific course and event delivery.Ties to the build-out roadmap's growing instructor bench, sourced from members and from outside.
  • Partner relationship management: identifying and tracking retail/commission partners and social media influencers, separate from instructors.A retail partner or influencer is a different kind of relationship than an instructor: no course to deliver, no availability to schedule, just a commercial arrangement. Managing both the same way loses that distinction.
  • Referral & commission tracking for vendor, outfitter, and influencer partners.The Business Model Canvas names this as how the connector role actually monetizes. It doesn't work without a way to attribute a signup to a specific partner.
  • Reporting that answers specific questions, not just totals.The roadmap already commits to tracking renewal/repeat-engagement rate and channel conversion rate at the end of Phase 2, not total sign-ups. Whatever system gets built needs to be able to produce those two numbers specifically.
  • Privacy & access controls over member data.This is a women's group whose members will be sharing location and meeting people in person. Visibility needs to default to controlled, not open.

Member What a member actually needs

  • Easy registration for classes, courses, and camps.
  • A clear view of membership benefits and what tier they're in.
  • Finding other members by interest, location, or experience level.
  • Talking to each other directly — chat or discussion, not just receiving Venery's email.
  • Organizing their own activities — a coffee, a hike, a hunt — without needing Venery to run it as a formal program.
  • Sharing resources and tips with each other, not only receiving content Alex wrote.
  • Access to course/curriculum content if it lives online rather than only live.
  • Control over their own visibility — deciding what other members can see about them.

PART 2 Data & Workflows

The actual pieces of information the system needs to hold, and the everyday sequences that use them. This is where "sounds right in principle" becomes "does this actually work when someone registers for a class."

Core data

One principle underneath everything below: a real person gets exactly one Person record, ever, no matter how many roles they take on. Membership, teaching, and volunteering are separate records that link to that one Person, not separate profiles that duplicate them. The same logic separates people from organizations: a company or nonprofit isn't a person with a business name, it's a different kind of record, and the two connect through their own link rather than a field on either side, since one organization can have several contacts and one person can represent more than one organization.

Person / Contact

The one record for every individual human Venery has any relationship with: a member, an instructor, a volunteer, a prospect. Membership and Instructor Engagement records link to this one; they never replace it.

FieldWhy it matters
Name, email, phone, mailing addressAddress is specifically the field Alex says is captured by Wix Events but never reaches the customer profile
How they found VeneryFeeds the channel-conversion metric the roadmap already committed to measuring
Date first engagedFirst-touch date, for measuring the actual funnel rather than guessing at it
Hunting experience levelBeginner / lapsed / already active — the segment distinction already on the canvas
Interest tagsDisciplines and topics (archery, rifle, food/butchering, policy, etc.) — what discovery is meant to surface
Marketing consent / opt-in statusNeeded regardless of tool, and currently the exact thing that falls through the cracks on unsubscribed registrants
Referred byLink to another Person, if word of mouth brought them in
Relationship notesFree text, the activity-tracking gap Klaviyo doesn't cover

Organization

A company, nonprofit, or agency Venery has a relationship with, not an individual. Keeps brands, outfitters, and conservation orgs from getting forced into Person fields that don't fit them. Who represents it is handled by Affiliation below, not a field here.

FieldWhy it matters
Organization name, typeVendor, outfitter, brand, nonprofit, agency, training company
NotesSame activity-tracking function as Person notes, for the organization as a whole

Affiliation

Links a Person to an Organization. Exists because a single "primary contact" field can't represent an outfitter with three different people Venery deals with, or an independent rep who represents several different brands at once. The same record works in both directions: who represents this organization, and which organizations this person represents.

FieldWhy it matters
Person (link), Organization (link)Both, always — this record only exists to connect the two
Role at that organizationOwner, marketing contact, logistics contact, sales rep
Primary contact (flag)Marks the preferred contact without forcing there to be only one
StatusActive / inactive — people change jobs; the Affiliation ends, the Person and Organization records don't

A role linked to an existing Person, not a separate profile. A member who starts teaching doesn't get duplicated: they get one of these attached to the Person record they already have. An outside expert who isn't a member gets a Person record too, just without a Membership attached to it.

Core workflows

Someone registers for a program solves Alex's #1 problem

  1. Person registers for an event on the website
  2. Their Contact record is created or matched automatically, capturing name, email, and address at the point of registration, not left behind in the event tool
  3. An Event Registration record links that Contact to that Event
  4. System checks: are they already a subscriber? If not, they're offered opt-in automatically, no manual step for Alex
  5. Payment is recorded as a transaction against their Contact record, not just inside the event app

Someone becomes a member

  1. Contact selects a tier and pays
  2. Membership record is created and linked to their Contact
  3. Tier benefits activate: discounts, content access, community platform access
  4. A renewal reminder is scheduled ahead of the renewal date

A member finds other members and organizes something

  1. Member completes a profile: interest tags, location, experience level
  2. They become discoverable to other members, within whatever privacy setting they chose
  3. They join or create an interest group, or propose a one-off meetup
  4. Other members RSVP; the organizer sees who's coming

An instructor relationship develops

  1. An exploratory conversation with a member, or with an outside expert who isn't one yet, is logged as an Instructor Engagement linked to their Person record, status: exploratory
  2. If it goes somewhere, status moves to active, with discipline and compensation captured
  3. They're assigned to specific Events; delivery is tracked against that assignment

A partner relationship develops, and a referral converts

  1. An exploratory conversation with a vendor, outfitter, or brand, whether that's a company or a single individual, is logged as a Partner Engagement linked to an Organization or a Person, status: exploratory
  2. If it goes somewhere, status moves to active and a referral code or link is issued
  3. A prospective member or customer uses that code at signup or registration
  4. The transaction is attributed back to that Partner Engagement automatically
  5. Commission is calculated once the structure (still open on the canvas) is decided

Measuring whether it's working feeds the roadmap's own checkpoint

  1. The system tracks last-active date, attendance frequency, and renewal outcome per Contact
  2. Reporting rolls this up into the two specific numbers the build-out roadmap already committed to: renewal/repeat-engagement rate and channel conversion rate
  3. That is the same data the roadmap's Measure & Decide checkpoint uses to decide whether to persevere or revisit, at the end of Phase 2
What's deliberately not here No platform names, no cost estimates, no build-vs-buy opinion. Once this list holds up in conversation with Alex, the next step is evaluating how existing tools, a different combination of them, or a custom-built approach each measure up against it, not the other way around.
